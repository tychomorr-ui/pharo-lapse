  Pharo-Lapse: Signed Store-and-Forward Delivery over Intermittent IP
                                Networks
                      draft-morris-pharo-lapse-00

Abstract

   Pharo-Lapse is a store-and-forward protocol for signed, pairwise
   message delivery between devices whose IP paths may be intermittent
   or unavailable.  It specifies durable endpoint and relay queues,
   content-addressed and Ed25519-signed envelopes, recipient
   deduplication, signed acknowledgments, bounded multi-hop forwarding,
   deterministic route selection, logical lapse ordering, and phase-
   anchored signatures.  It operates above a carrier that provides IP
   connectivity and does not require the public Internet, a cloud
   broker, a certificate authority, or a central routing service.  This
   document defines version 1.1.0 of the protocol and its direct-
   delivery compatibility behavior for version 1.0 peers.

1.  Introduction and Scope

   Conventional delivery systems commonly depend on a continuously
   available path, a reachable server, or a certificate authority.
   Pharo-Lapse instead treats a path as an opportunity that may appear
   and disappear.  A sender, recipient, and an optional transit peer
   retain the state needed to retry delivery after interruption.

   The protocol is above the transport and below an application.  It can
   use Wi-Fi, Ethernet, cellular, satellite, or local mesh radio when
   that carrier offers IP connectivity.  A WireGuard overlay is one
   supported carrier configuration; the protocol does not define or
   replace WireGuard.  Every peer is symmetric and can initiate delivery
   to a pinned peer with an out-of-band-established overlay address.

   Pharo-Lapse provides end-to-end integrity and provenance, durable
   store-and-forward delivery, bounded forwarding, and at-most-once
   recipient application processing.  It does not provide
   confidentiality, anonymity, broadcast or multicast, real-time
   delivery guarantees, a distributed consensus mechanism, endpoint-
   compromise resistance, or transport denial-of-service prevention.
   Transport-layer confidentiality, when needed, is supplied separately.

1.1.  Design Goals

   *  Messages submitted to an outbox are eventually acknowledged or
      placed in a policy terminal state; they are not silently dropped.

   *  A recipient processes a given msg_id at most once despite
      retransmission or forwarding.

   *  An origin signature binds the sender, final recipient, payload,
      message identifier, phase anchor, and origin route parameters.

   *  A permitted relay can forward independently of a broker,
      certificate authority, or central routing service.

   *  Routes are bounded and loop-free, and delivery decisions are
      reproducible from the same canonical state, phase, and lapse.

   *  Process death, crash, and restart do not cause loss or duplicate
      application processing.

2.  Conventions and Terminology

   The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, and MAY in this
   document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC2119].

   Peer:  A device with a canonical identity: a stable peer_id and long-
      term Ed25519 public key.

   Envelope:  The signed wire-format message specified in Section 4.

   Origin envelope:  The sender-created immutable message content and
      sender signature.

   Forwarding record:  A relay's signed, append-only acceptance record
      for another peer's envelope.

   Outbox:  A durable on-disk queue for messages awaiting delivery or
      acknowledgment.

   Inbox:  A durable table for messages received and verified at least
      once.

   Ack:  A signed envelope of type ack returned by the final recipient
      after successful delivery.

   Lapse:  A monotonic logical ordering unit local to a peer.
      Operations in one lapse are simultaneous; different lapses are
      ordered.



Morris                        Informational                     [Page 3]


                               Pharo-Lapse                  October 2026


   Phase:  The mesh operating mode DAY, NIGHT, or TWILIGHT, derived from
      observed peer reachability rather than a wall clock.

   CID:  A Content Identifier.  This protocol uses CIDv1 with dag-json
      codec and sha2-256 multihash.

   Origin CID:  The CID of immutable origin content and its sender
      signature.  It remains stable across forwarding.

   Pin:  A public key stored locally by an operator for verification of
      inbound signatures.  Pins are established out of band.

   Transit peer:  A relay that accepts and forwards a valid envelope
      without becoming the final recipient.

3.  Architecture and Peer Configuration

   A local peer configuration maps peer_id to overlay IP address, port,
   and pinned public key.  The mapping and pins are established out of
   band.  A direct delivery has one origin-to-recipient path.  A multi-
   hop delivery has one or more transit peers.  Before attempting a next
   hop, a transit peer durably stores the full envelope and its own
   forwarding record.  It verifies, but neither decrypts nor re-signs,
   origin content.

   The reference deployment uses HTTP/1.0 over a WireGuard overlay.
   HTTP/1.1 or HTTP/2 can be used instead.  The only required
   application endpoint is a POST to /inbox.  WireGuard uses Curve25519/
   Noise-based secure transport construction and UDP encapsulation; its
   transport security is separate from the envelope-level signatures and
   content addressing in this document [WIREGUARD].  Implementations are
   not constrained to one storage engine, programming language, or HTTP
   version.

4.  Envelope Wire Format

4.1.  Envelope Fields and Version Behavior

   An envelope is a canonical JSON object.  The following fields form
   version 1.1.0.  A recipient validates the types and bounds in
   addition to the signing and CID rules below.

   msg_id:  Required UUID version 4 string uniquely identifying a
      message.

   created_at:  Required ISO 8601 UTC creation timestamp.

   sender_peer_id and recipient_peer_id:  Required canonical origin and



Morris                        Informational                     [Page 4]


                               Pharo-Lapse                  October 2026


      final-recipient identities.

   body:  Required message payload string of at most 4096 characters.

   phase:  Required DAY, NIGHT, or TWILIGHT string.

   lapse:  Required integer, monotonic for the sender.

   phase_anchor:  Required in version 1.1.0.  It is PHARO-LAPSE-
      PHASE/1|<phase>|<lapse>.

   prev_cid:  Required CID of the sender's prior envelope, or JSON null
      for its first envelope.

   type:  Required value message, ack, or ping.

   route_history:  Required in version 1.1.0 ordered array of accepting
      peer IDs, beginning with the origin sender.

   max_hops:  Required in version 1.1.0 integer from 1 through 64.  It
      is the maximum number of transit acceptances after the origin.

   origin_cid:  Required in version 1.1.0 stable CID of immutable origin
      content.

   sig:  Required base64-encoded Ed25519 origin signature over the
      origin signing domain.

   forwarding:  Optional append-only array with one signed record per
      transit acceptance.

   cid:  Required CIDv1 of the complete current envelope excluding this
      field.

   hw_bound:  Required when the identity key was generated in software
      because no suitable hardware module was available.  It records
      false in that case.  A receiving peer can apply stricter policy to
      this condition.

   A version 1.0 peer that does not understand an extension MUST treat
   an envelope as a version 1.0 direct-delivery envelope only when both
   route_history and max_hops are absent.  Otherwise, it MUST return an
   unsupported-extension error without processing the body.  Version 1.0
   defaults are phase_anchor = PHARO-LAPSE-PHASE/1|<phase>|<lapse>,
   route_history = [sender_peer_id], max_hops = 1, and origin_cid = cid
   computed over the version 1.0 envelope.  A version 1.1.0
   implementation MUST NOT silently apply those defaults to a malformed
   version 1.1.0 envelope that advertises an unsupported version.

4.2.  Canonical JSON

   All object keys are sorted lexicographically by Unicode code point.
   The encoding is UTF-8.  There is no insignificant whitespace.  Non-
   ASCII characters remain UTF-8.  Numbers use ECMAScript
   Number.prototype.toString() representation for the values used by
   this protocol, and arrays retain their order.  An implementation MUST
   reject duplicate JSON object keys before canonicalization.  Every
   signature and CID calculation uses this exact serialization.

4.3.  Signing and CID Domains

   The origin signing domain is the canonical object containing every
   envelope field except sig, cid, forwarding, and route_history.  It
   includes phase, lapse, phase_anchor, max_hops, and origin_cid when
   present.  The origin signature therefore binds the message, final
   recipient, phase, lapse, and hop budget but not mutable transit
   history.

   For version 1.1.0, origin_cid is the CIDv1 of the origin signing-
   domain object together with origin route_history and max_hops, before
   a forwarding record is added.  It MUST remain unchanged at every hop.
   The current envelope CID is the CIDv1 dag-json sha2-256 digest of the
   complete current envelope without the cid field.  A relay MUST
   recompute the current CID after adding a forwarding record, and a
   recipient MUST verify both CIDs.  CIDv1 is a self-describing,
   cryptographically content-addressed identifier [CID].  A version 1.0
   envelope retains its version 1.0 signing and CID input: its complete
   envelope without cid.

4.4.  Acknowledgment Envelope

   An ack has type equal to ack.  Its body contains acked_msg_id,
   acked_origin_cid, stored_at, and route_history.  The final recipient
   signs it.  The sender MUST verify the ack signature against the
   pinned recipient key and MUST verify that acked_msg_id,
   acked_origin_cid, and the route binding match the outbound message.

4.5.  Forwarding Record

   A forwarding record has peer_id, accepted_at as an ISO 8601 UTC
   timestamp, relay lapse, previous_cid, next_peer_id, the relay phase
   anchor, and a base64 Ed25519 sig.  Its signature covers the record
   without sig using the relay's pinned identity.  A record is appended
   atomically with the relay's durable queue insert.

   A relay MUST NOT modify body, sender_peer_id, recipient_peer_id,
   phase, phase_anchor, lapse, prev_cid, type, max_hops, or origin_cid.

5.  Identity and Integrity Rules

   Every conforming implementation applies the following rules.

5.1.  Hardware-Bound Identity

   A peer MUST generate its long-term Ed25519 keypair in a hardware
   security module, TPM, StrongBox, Secure Enclave, or equivalent when
   one is available.  The private key MUST NOT be held in general-
   purpose memory or storage, and signing operations MUST be performed
   by that module.  If no such module is available, software generation
   is permitted only with the hw_bound: false indication described in
   Section 4.1.  Peers can enforce stricter local policy for non-
   hardware-bound identities.

5.2.  Signed and Content-Addressed Messages

   Every envelope MUST carry a valid origin signature and a CID.  The
   recipient verifies the origin signature using the sender's pin before
   application processing, recomputes both origin_cid and current cid,
   and compares them.  On failure, the recipient MUST discard the
   envelope and return an error response.  Ed25519 signing and
   verification are specified by [RFC8032].

6.  Durable Queues, Retry, and Deduplication

6.1.  Queue and Retry Requirements

   Every peer MUST maintain durable outbox and inbox storage.  A transit
   peer MUST write an accepted envelope and forwarding record before the
   next-hop attempt.  On process start, every outbox entry in inflight
   state MUST reset to pending and be retried.

   The retry schedule MUST be non-decreasing and MUST NOT retry a given
   message faster than once every 10 seconds.  The reference schedule is
   30, 60, 120, 240, 480, and 600 seconds with plus-or-minus 10 percent
   jitter, capped at 600 seconds.  An implementation can use another
   schedule that meets these properties.  The maximum attempt count
   SHOULD be 20, after which a message MUST be marked dead and must not
   be automatically retried.  The default message TTL SHOULD be 72
   hours; after expiry it MUST be marked expired.

   Outbox states are pending (waiting for its next attempt), inflight
   (currently transmitting), acked (terminal delivery confirmation),
   dead (terminal failure or maximum attempts), and expired (terminal
   TTL expiry).  A transit queue retains the origin CID, current
   envelope CID, next peer, and forwarding record that created its
   entry.  Deleting a transit record before final ack is non-conforming.

6.2.  Deduplication

   The final recipient MUST keep a persistent inbox keyed by msg_id.  A
   transit peer MUST keep a persistent transit table keyed by the tuple
   (msg_id, origin_cid).  A peer performs its applicable lookup before
   expensive signature verification.  A duplicate final delivery returns
   the cached ack; a duplicate transit delivery returns the cached
   forwarding result.  A duplicate MUST NOT invoke application
   processing or a second forwarding action.

6.3.  Crash Recovery

   Before any other process-start operation, the outbox applies the
   equivalent of the following statements:

   UPDATE outbox
   SET state = 'pending', last_error = 'recovered from crash'
   WHERE state = 'inflight';

   UPDATE outbox
   SET state = 'expired'
   WHERE state = 'pending' AND expires_at < now();

   Transit stores apply equivalent recovery to forwarding records whose
   next-hop attempt was interrupted.  This ensures interrupted messages
   are retried and messages past TTL are not retried.

7.  Forwarding, Lapse, Phase, and Routes

7.1.  Bounded, Loop-Free Forwarding

   A peer can act as a transit peer only when its registry permits
   transit and it has a reachable next hop.  Before accepting a transit
   envelope, it MUST verify the origin signature, origin CID, current
   CID, and all prior forwarding records; confirm that the local peer is
   absent from route_history; confirm that len(route_history) - 1 <
   max_hops before local acceptance; confirm that the proposed next peer
   is absent from the route and is not an excluded peer under applicable
   recipient policy; and confirm that the envelope's recipient is not
   local unless this is final delivery.

   The relay appends its own ID and exactly one forwarding record,
   persists the new current CID, and queues the next-hop transmission.
   It MUST reject a repeated route with LOOP_DETECTED and a route beyond
   budget with MAX_HOPS_EXCEEDED.  Such a rejection MUST NOT mutate the
   application inbox.

7.2.  Phase Model and Phase-Anchored Signing

   Phase is the local peer's observed reachability state, not a wall-
   clock property.  Let P be its pinned peers and R the members of P
   responding to keepalives in the preceding 60 seconds.  The phase is
   DAY if R/P is at least 0.5, NIGHT if R/P is less than 0.25, and
   TWILIGHT otherwise.  The reference probe intervals are 25 seconds for
   DAY, 300 seconds for NIGHT, and 120 seconds for TWILIGHT.  NIGHT
   applies a long retry schedule of five times the base; DAY uses a
   short schedule and TWILIGHT an intermediate schedule.

   Phase transitions MUST be hysteretic.  A single missing response MUST
   NOT cause a DAY-to-NIGHT transition.  The reference implementation
   uses three consecutive below-threshold lapses to enter NIGHT and one
   successful pinned-peer response to move from NIGHT to DAY.

   An origin envelope MUST contain exactly PHARO-LAPSE-
   PHASE/1|<phase>|<lapse> in phase_anchor.  The origin signature covers
   the phase, lapse, and anchor together, and a verifier MUST reject a
   non-recomputing string.  A forwarding record similarly contains and
   signs its relay phase anchor using its own lapse and observed phase.
   A relay MUST NOT rewrite an origin phase or lapse; it records its own
   phase only in the forwarding record and applies that phase to local
   next-hop scheduling.

7.3.  Lapse and Route Discipline

   A lapse increments on each successful delivery attempt, each received
   envelope, and each phase transition.  It is a peer-local logical
   clock; peers can have different lapse values at the same wall-clock
   moment.  Envelopes from the same sender MUST have strictly increasing
   lapse.  An envelope whose lapse is less than or equal to the highest
   previous lapse from that sender is a stale replay and MUST be
   rejected, except a duplicate with a previously accepted msg_id
   follows Section 6.2.  The lapse at signing is included in every
   receipt; replaying a receipt at a different lapse is invalid.

   The first route-history entry MUST equal sender_peer_id.  Each
   transit acceptance appends exactly one ID.  The final recipient can
   append its ID while storing the message, but if it does it MUST do so
   before calculating the ack route binding.  An ID can appear at most
   once.  The max_hops value counts transit acceptances after the origin
   and before final delivery: a value of 1 permits direct delivery only,
   while a sender wanting one relay sets it to at least 2.  A route is
   invalid if len(route_history) - 1 > max_hops, the local peer is
   already present, or the next peer is already present.  An invalid
   route is rejected without forwarding.

7.4.  Peer Registry Extension and Route Selection

   A version 1.1.0 peer record contains peer_id, host, port,
   public_key_b64, via_peer_ids, and last_seen.  The via_peer_ids field
   is an ordered list of peers through which the address is reachable.
   It is a routing hint rather than authorization.  An empty or absent
   value means direct reachability is expected.  Registry data by itself
   does not establish identity; entries MUST be locally pinned or
   authenticated out of band.

   A peer MUST NOT select a route with a repeated ID.  When multiple
   routes are available, it filters routes violating the preceding route
   rules, selects the fewest remaining hops, and breaks a tie using
   lexicographic order of the complete peer-ID path.  Reachability and
   phase metrics can first remove unavailable routes, but cannot change
   this deterministic tie-break among remaining paths.

8.  Delivery Algorithm and Recipient Processing

8.1.  Sender Loop and Delivery Attempt

   The sender selects up to ten outbox entries in pending state whose
   next_attempt_at is absent or due, ordered by creation time.  It marks
   each candidate inflight, attempts delivery or forwarding, and handles
   the result.  In one-shot operation, no candidates terminates the
   loop; otherwise the peer waits and continues.

   For a delivery attempt, the peer looks up the destination and
   via_peer_ids, selects a valid deterministic route, and POSTs the
   current envelope to http://peer.host:peer.port/inbox with a 30-second
   timeout.  A timeout, connection refusal, or other transport failure
   is a RETRYABLE_ERROR.  For a 200 response, the sender parses the ack
   and verifies the pinned recipient signature, acked_msg_id,
   acked_origin_cid, and route binding.

8.2.  Transit and Recipient Actions

   A transit peer verifies the origin and every forwarding record,
   applies loop and hop checks, durably writes its transit record,
   appends its forwarding record, recomputes the current CID, and queues
   the next hop.  It returns a signed forwarding receipt only after the
   durable write succeeds.  If no valid next hop exists, it returns
   NO_ROUTE and retains the message in pending until policy marks it
   dead or expired.

   On POST to /inbox, a recipient parses JSON and performs the
   applicable dedup lookup first.  For a nonduplicate it verifies the
   origin signature and forwarding signatures; recomputes and compares



Morris                        Informational                    [Page 10]


                               Pharo-Lapse                  October 2026


   CIDs; checks recipient match, phase anchor, lapse monotonicity, route
   history, hop budget, type, and body length; then starts a
   transaction.  The transaction inserts the inbox record, updates the
   peer's last lapse, and persists the cached ack before commit.  It
   returns 200 with canonical ack JSON only after a successful commit.

8.3.  Result Handling

   SUCCESS(ack) marks an entry acked and stores the ack.
   RETRYABLE_ERROR increments attempts, computes the next delay, and
   returns the entry to pending.  HARD_ERROR marks it dead and records
   the reason.  LOOP_DETECTED, MAX_HOPS_EXCEEDED, INVALID_ORIGIN, and
   INVALID_FORWARDING_RECORD are hard errors.  An entry reaching 20
   attempts is dead regardless of other outcome handling.

9.  Receipt Format and External Provenance

   Every successful final delivery produces a signed recipient ack.  The
   sender and transit peers can additionally create outbound or
   forwarding receipts.  An outbound receipt contains receipt_id (UUID
   v4), msg_id, sender and recipient peer IDs, input_hash (sha256 of
   canonical origin envelope), origin_cid, state_hash (sha256 of outbox
   state), rule_set_version equal to PHARO-LAPSE-1.1.0, authority_state
   equal to NOT_CONFIGURED or OPERATOR_APPROVED, model_inference equal
   to false, external_ai equal to false, phase, lapse, route history,
   max hops, result_state equal to acked, queued, dead, or expired, and
   an Ed25519 signature over canonical JSON.

   Given the same input envelope, outbox state, phase, lapse, and route
   history, a receipt MUST be byte-identical.  OpenTimestamps proofs can
   be retained separately to provide externally verifiable evidence that
   a file existed before an attested time [OTS].  Such a proof is not
   part of envelope validity, receipt validity, message delivery, or the
   protocol's security model.

10.  Compatibility, Implementation, and Verification Requirements

10.1.  Historical Gate Mapping

   Version 1.1.0 retains version 1.0 direct-path compatibility.  For the
   historical transport gate, a valid version 1.1.0 receipt must show an
   actual HTTP exchange over a WireGuard overlay while public Internet
   access is unavailable.  For the signed-envelope and ack gate, direct
   delivery remains compatible and verifies phase_anchor where version
   1.1.0 extensions are present.  For crash recovery, senders and
   transit peers recover inflight entries to pending, and a transit
   crash does not create two forwarding records or two next-hop
   application actions.  For exactly-once delivery, final recipients



Morris                        Informational                    [Page 11]


                               Pharo-Lapse                  October 2026


   deduplicate by msg_id and transit peers deduplicate by (msg_id,
   origin_cid) while returning a cached forwarding result without
   forwarding twice.

   Historical version 1.0 gate receipts are not executable tests and
   cannot be represented as fresh version 1.1.0 executions.  A future
   live version 1.1.0 gate receipt must contain its commit hash, test
   command, environment, message IDs, CIDs, route history, and UTC
   timestamps.

10.2.  Executable Test Requirements

   A version 1.1.0 implementation MUST provide executable tests for
   direct version 1.0-compatible delivery with absent extensions; phase-
   anchor mismatch rejection; origin-signature rejection after body,
   phase, lapse, or hop-budget mutation; one-relay forwarding with
   stable origin CID and changed current CID; forwarding-signature
   verification; repeated-peer loop rejection; hop-budget rejection;
   transit crash recovery without duplicate forwarding; final-recipient
   deduplication with cached ack; deterministic route selection; parsing
   of registry via_peer_ids; and backward-compatible direct delivery
   under the four historical gates.

10.3.  Implementation Notes

   The reference implementation uses SQLite with WAL journaling.  Its
   outbox and inbox are single tables keyed by msg_id.  A version 1.1.0
   implementation adds transit records, forwarding records, origin CID,
   current CID, max hops, route history, and via_peer_ids.  Indexes on
   state, next-attempt time, peer ID, and origin CID support the
   delivery loop.  A single-threaded loop is permitted; concurrent peer
   delivery is optional.

11.  Security Considerations

   Pharo-Lapse assumes a passive transport eavesdropper, an active man-
   in-the-middle, replay attempts, impersonation attempts, and crashes
   at arbitrary points.  It relies on transport for confidentiality,
   envelope and forwarding signatures plus CID verification for
   integrity, deduplication and lapse monotonicity for replay handling,
   local pins for identity verification, route history and hop budget
   for loop prevention, and durable recovery for crashes.

   It does not assume resilience to endpoint compromise, transport
   denial of service, or operator error.  Physical seizure, endpoint
   compromise, theft of a software-held key, revocation lag, side-
   channel leakage, route exhaustion, and denial of service remain
   residual risks.  Implementations SHOULD use hardware-backed keys,
   signed pin updates, per-peer rate limits, queue-size caps, and
   operator-visible route-rejection logs.

   The design eliminates a prompt channel and shell invocation from
   protocol processing.  SQL operations use parameterized queries.
   Origin and current CID checks protect against modification, and no
   central authority creates a central-compromise dependency.
   Implementers are responsible for safe JSON parsing, duplicate-key
   rejection, constant-time cryptographic implementations where
   applicable, secure private-key operations, durable storage behavior,
   and transport configuration.  Ed25519 relies on [RFC8032]; transports
   based on Curve25519 use the functions specified in [RFC7748] where
   applicable.

13.  Implementation and Publication Status

   This document describes the complete local version 1.1.0 protocol
   specification.  The repository's historical receipts are evidence for
   version 1.0 runs only.  At this document's preparation, no executable
   harness and two-device overlay were available to re-run the gates
   under version 1.1.0 semantics.  No live version 1.1.0 test,
   deployment, telemetry record, IETF submission, or publication is
   asserted by this document.
